The problem

Your class has a syllabus. The chatbot doesn’t.

Students already use AI for homework and exam prep — that ship has sailed. But a general model has never seen your curriculum, your teacher’s notes, or the way your school explains a topic. So the help arrives generic, occasionally confidently wrong, and in a form a student has no way to check.

A teacher can’t vouch for an answer whose source they cannot see. That is the gap this lab exists to close.

What makes it different

Three rules, and the third one is the point.

Grounded

Answers are retrieved from teacher-approved school materials — the notes and topics your class actually uses — not from the open web.

Cited

Every answer carries the source it leaned on, openable in one click. A student can check it; a teacher can audit it.

Bounded

When the approved sources don’t cover a question, it refuses. Saying “I don’t know” is a designed outcome here, not a failure.

How an answer is produced

Retrieve first. Then answer. Or don’t.

Four steps, in this order. The order is the safety property — the model never gets to answer before the school’s own materials have been consulted.

STEP 01

Student asks

A homework or exam-prep question, in the context of a real class.

STEP 02

Retrieve

Search the teacher-approved material for passages that actually bear on the question.

STEP 03

Generate

The model writes an answer conditioned on those passages — not on its own recollection.

STEP 04

Cite

The answer ships with the sources it used, open for the student to read.

!

And when the sources aren’t enough

The chain stops. No approved source, no confident answer — the one rule everything else here is built around.

Where we actually are

A prototype, honestly labelled.

This is a lab, not a launch. We would rather show you the gaps than a demo that hides them — so here is the real state of the build, with nothing rounded up.

Closed web prototype

Chat interface, openable sources, usefulness rating, session export. Real and running today.

Retrieval

Works — but over demo notes, not yet the approved Quantum STEM corpus.

Connected LLM on school content

Not yet. Source-grounded answers on real school material are the goal of v0.2.

Teacher-validated quality

Not yet. No evaluation scores exist, so none are published.

Student pilot

Not yet. Only after a school agreement and a written data policy.

Production deployment

No. Nothing here is production, and we don’t claim product–market fit.

Who this is for

One school at a time, starting with one subject.

The scope is deliberately small. A single school keeps the knowledge base auditable, and a single subject keeps the first evaluation honest.
